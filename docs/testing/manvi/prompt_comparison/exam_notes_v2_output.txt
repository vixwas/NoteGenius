## 3. Important Definitions

**Important Definition: Newton's Second Law of Motion**

> The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.

**Important Definition: One Newton (N)**

> One Newton is defined as the magnitude of force required to accelerate a mass of one kilogram (1 kg) at a rate of one metre per second squared (1 m/s²).

## 4. Concept Explanations

**Key Concept: Momentum and Force Relationship**

- Force is not merely a cause of motion, but a cause of *change* in motion (specifically, a change in momentum).
- The vector nature of the law implies that the acceleration vector (a⃗) is always in the exact same direction as the net applied force vector (F⃗).

**Key Concept: The Constant Mass Condition**

- In introductory classical mechanics, mass (m) is generally assumed to be constant over time.
- Under this specific condition, the general statement of the law simplifies significantly to relate force directly to acceleration (F = ma).
- Note: For variable-mass systems, such as burning rockets, the full momentum formulation must be utilized, though only the constant-mass form is detailed in the primary source.

## 5. Formulas

**Important Formula: General Momentum Form**

F⃗ ∝ d p⃗ / dt

Where p⃗ is linear momentum (p⃗ = m v⃗).

**Important Formula: Constant Mass Form**

F⃗ = m a⃗

**Symbol Explanations:**

- F⃗ = Applied Force (measured in Newtons, N)
- m = Mass of the object (measured in kilograms, kg)
- a⃗ = Acceleration produced (measured in metres per second squared, m/s²)

## 6. Examples

**Important Example: Calculating Acceleration**

**Problem:** If a force of 10 N acts on an object of mass 2 kg, calculate its acceleration.

**Given:**
- Force, F = 10 N
- Mass, m = 2 kg

**Formula:**

a = F / m

**Calculation:**

a = 10 N / 2 kg = 5 m/s²

**Answer:** The acceleration of the object is 5 m/s².

## 7. Comparisons

**Note:** While the provided source material focuses exclusively on Newton's Second Law, a standard academic comparison format is provided below to contrast it with Newton's First Law for examination completeness, strictly adhering to fundamental mechanics principles.

| Feature | Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia) | Newton's Second Law (Law of Force & Acceleration) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Focus** | Qualitative definition of force and inertia. | Quantitative measurement of force. |
| **Mathematical Equation** | ΣF⃗ = 0 (when equilibrium exists). | F⃗ = ma⃗ |
| **Condition** | Zero net force (constant velocity or rest). | Non-zero net force (resulting in acceleration). |

## 8. Applications

- Designing safety features (e.g., seatbelts, airbags) by analyzing impact forces and momentum changes.
- Solving dynamic mechanical systems using free-body diagrams and equations of motion (F = ma).

## 9. Quick-Revision Summary

- **Core Law:** Rate of change of momentum is proportional to applied force and acts in its direction.
- **Governing Equation:** F = ma (assuming constant mass).
- **SI Unit:** Newton (N), equivalent to kg·m/s².
- **Definition of 1 N:** Force required to accelerate 1 kg at 1 m/s².
