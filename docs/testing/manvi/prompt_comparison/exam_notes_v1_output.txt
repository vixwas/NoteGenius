# Examination Notes: Newton's Second Law of Motion

## 1. Important Definitions

- Newton's Second Law of Motion: States that the rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.

- Newton (N) [SI Unit of Force]: Defined as the force required to accelerate a mass of one kilogram (1 kg) at the rate of one metre per second squared (1 m/s²).

## 2. Important Concepts

### 2.1 Core Principles

- Relationship between Force and Momentum: The fundamental premise connects applied force directly to how rapidly an object's momentum changes.
- Directionality: The resulting acceleration and change in momentum occur in the direction of the applied force.
- Condition for F = ma: The simplified equation assumes constant mass.

### 2.2 Mathematical Formulation

F = ma

Where:
- F = Force applied
- m = Mass of the object
- a = Acceleration produced

## 3. Formulas and Numericals

### 3.1 Mathematical Expression

Force Equation: F = ma

Rearranged for Acceleration: a = F/m

### 3.2 Numerical Example

Given:
- Force = 10 N
- Mass = 2 kg

Calculation:
a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s²

Result: Acceleration = 5 m/s².

## 4. Exam-Oriented Content

- Key terminology: Rate of change of momentum, applied force, constant mass, acceleration, Newton.
- Core exam takeaway: Understand the rate of change of momentum and apply F = ma.
- SI units: kg, m/s² and N = kg·m/s².

## 5. Revision Section

### Important Definitions
- Newton's Second Law: Rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to applied force.
- 1 Newton: Force needed to give a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

### Important Formulas

- Force determines the direction of acceleration.
